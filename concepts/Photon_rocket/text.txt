Photon rocket engine

The problem: normal fuel rockets works fine for travelling t other planets or its moon in our solar system, but we face a physical bottleneck when it's about interstellar travel to maybe different star, we can carry a limited amount of fuel in the rocket, the fuel no matter how much will run out after a while, and large amount of fuels are also harder to get outside of earths atmosphere, all of these problems limit our dreams to fly to different star in practical timeframe.

So I thought why not get rid chemical fuels and maybe rely on electricity because sufficient amount of electricity can be generated by nuclear reactors reliably for long duration of time. But the problem is to gain momentum in space we have to push against something, in case of chemical fuels we pushed gas to accelerate, but for electricity? There exists only one option (as far as I know) and that is photons,  so the photon rocket engine is basically a rocket engine that creates a large amount of photons and expel them and since photons carry momentum we get a very small acceleration from each photon. We need a lot of photons to get a considerable amount of acceleration.


According to calculations the issue is we need a significant amount of energy about 12.1 tera watts

We have a few options 
1. Put the nuclear reactors at earth and beam a laser to the spacecraft, but it will be a one way trip then
2. Another option is we use matter antimatter annihilation to generate the photons , a problem might arise how will we store antimatter for years on a spacecraft making sure it doesn't annihilate the spacecraft itself, the solution maybe, we don't store the antimatter we create it on the trip continuously.
